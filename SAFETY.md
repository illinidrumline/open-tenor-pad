# Safety

Building a practice pad involves wood dust, rubber, and solvent adhesives. Nothing here is a
consumer product, and nothing here replaces your own training or your local regulations.

## Shop hazards

| Hazard | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| MDF/HDF dust (respirable, binder-containing) | dust extraction plus an N95 or better respirator; cut outdoors or in a ventilated space |
| Contact cement and solvent fumes (flammable, neurotoxic) | use outdoors or with cross-ventilation; no ignition sources; nitrile gloves; let it flash off before joining |
| Cutting, drilling, routing | eye protection, push sticks, secured workpieces; no gloves near spinning tools |
| Hearing | a struck pad is loud in a closed room; use hearing protection for long sessions |
| Pinching and crushing | clamp the work before drilling; mind the drill bit exit |

## Material handling

- Natural gum rubber oxidizes. Store finished pads out of direct sunlight and away from ozone
  sources. Expect gradual hardening over years; that is a property of the material, not a defect.
- Contact cement bonds are permanent. Dry-fit before applying adhesive; there is no repositioning.
- Keep adhesive and solvent containers closed when not in use.

## Limits of this design

- This pad is a practice instrument. It is not a load-bearing, structural, or safety-critical item.
- The threaded insert is for a cymbal stand or a snare basket only. Do not hang anything heavier
  than the pad from it.
- Do not modify the design in ways that trap fingers or expose sharp fastener ends.
